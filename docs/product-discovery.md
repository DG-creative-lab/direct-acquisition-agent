# Design-partner brief and demand discovery

Working product name: GTM Agent Platform.
Status: positioning hypotheses and draft pitch, 27 November 2026.
Use the [product overview](product.md) for current capability limits and the
[platform map](gtm-platform-map.md) for architecture and delivery order.

This brief supports early conversations while the product is being built.
It is not a public launch announcement, a commitment to delivery dates or
evidence of product-market fit.

## Short introduction

> We're building an agent-led workspace that helps a team turn product knowledge
> into reviewable campaigns and use the results to decide what to try next.
> We're starting with contextual YouTube campaigns for products people can try
> through a demo: recommend creative/context matches within a campaign budget,
> then use qualified outcomes to inform the next allocation. The longer-term
> direction brings direct outreach and agentic commerce into the same
> knowledge, review and learning workflow.

## A concrete first conversation

> Imagine a voice-product team preparing a small YouTube campaign for video
> editors and narrators. They want suitable contexts, creative that fits those
> contexts and a clear way to measure qualified demo use. We want the agent to
> carry the product brief through that work, show a concrete proposal for review
> and use observed results to inform the next experiment.
>
> We already have a local prototype for authored campaign preparation, exact
> review and manual export, plus separate offline decisioning tools. Trusted
> measurement and the complete hosted workflow are still being built. I'd like
> to understand how your team handles this today and whether one part would be
> useful enough to test together.

The example is an illustrative use case, not an installed live targeting
integration or a claim of a relationship with a particular company.
Use an owned, permitted example for a demonstration.

## Initial customer hypothesis

Start discovery with AI or developer-tool product teams that have:

- A product action that can be defined and observed, such as a completed useful
  demo, with a clear distinction between a visit and a qualified result.
- A named acquisition owner and someone able to review product claims/creative.
- A recent campaign or upcoming experiment whose existing workflow they can explain.
- A plausible way to evaluate a contextual campaign, whether handled internally
  or with an agency.

This is a focus for learning, not a measured market segment. Do not require
every prospect to fit the full future platform. Agencies may be users, partners
or a different buyer segment; record that distinction rather than mixing their
responses with product-team demand.

## Value hypotheses to test

| Hypothesis | What would support it | What would challenge it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Carrying product knowledge into proposals reduces review friction | A recent example of repeated corrections or lost context; a reviewer finds the proposal easier to assess | The existing process already carries context accurately with little effort |
| A contextual YouTube proposal is a useful first deliverable | An acquisition owner supplies a permitted task and wants to review a second proposal | The team does not use or cannot evaluate that channel; another task is consistently more urgent |
| Evidence-backed next experiments matter | Someone owns the outcome and can explain how it would change the next campaign | No usable outcome definition, access or decision owner exists |
| Review and manual export are a useful initial boundary | The team can use an approved artifact in its current workflow | Value depends on unavailable live execution or integrations |
| A customer could buy a focused channel capability | A budget owner can describe a buying decision and a concrete trial criterion | Interest remains general enthusiasm with no owner, urgency or follow-up |

These observations guide product choices. They are not proof that the agent
improves conversions or saves money.

## Discovery conversation

Begin with the prospect's work before presenting the full platform:

1. Walk me through your last comparable campaign or product-discovery experiment.
   What triggered it, who prepared it, and who approved it?
2. Where did the product facts, audience/context choices and creative come from?
   Which handoff required the most correction or waiting?
3. What did you use to decide whether it worked? What evidence was missing?
4. What tools, agencies or internal effort do you use today? Who owns that budget?
5. If we could test one narrow task, which result would be useful enough to use?
   What would make it unacceptable?
6. Who would review the output, what approved material could they supply, and
   when could we meet again to assess it?

Then show the relevant local workflow and ask what is missing or unnecessary.
Ask separately which integrations are essential for a trial and which can wait.
Record requests for other channels as evidence; do not promise them during the call.

Avoid using "Would you use an AI marketing platform?" as the main demand signal.
Ask for a recent problem, an observable next action and the person responsible.

## What can be demonstrated now

Use the local YouTube [preparation guide](youtube-preparation.md) to show:

1. A supported authored request and its draft permission.
2. The placement/creative package, source labels and bundled comparison media.
3. An edit that produces a new exact revision.
4. Human approval and the corresponding manual export.
5. What the next measurement step must establish.

Label the catalogue as fictional and the comparison media as prerecorded.
Show the contextual benchmark separately, if relevant, with its authored inputs
and limits. Do not present its rankings as verified live placements, measured
campaign performance or a currently executing allocator.

A separate synthetic postal/email rehearsal can explain review and recovery.
The external outcomes registry illustrates operator-attested evidence intake;
it does not prove authenticated reporting or automatically improve chat output.

## The initial ask

> Would you spend 30 minutes walking through a recent campaign with us and
> reviewing one prototype task? If it is useful, we can agree a narrowly scoped
> trial with a named owner, a result you would use and a way to judge it.

A first round of five focused conversations is a practical starting batch, not
a statistically representative sample or a success threshold. Seek a second
working session or an agreed task as well as verbal interest. A paid pilot is
a separate commercial decision, not something this brief has priced or promised.

For a possible trial, agree the task, approved source material, reviewer,
baseline workflow, acceptance criteria, access boundaries and follow-up date.
A standalone online trial depends on the unfinished hosted release. An initial
local walkthrough can proceed without pretending that release exists.

## Measure demand and product usefulness separately

| Question | Evidence to collect |
| --- | --- |
| Is this an urgent problem? | Recent example, frequency, consequence and current workaround |
| Is there a buyer? | Named owner, budget process, decision constraints and willingness to agree a concrete next step |
| Is the output useful? | Reviewer acceptance/corrections against agreed criteria and whether they would use the exported result |
| Does it reduce preparation effort? | Comparable time/effort observations under a defined task, including human correction |
| Does it improve acquisition? | A separate correctly measured campaign experiment with an eligible denominator, comparison and uncertainty |

Keep an evidence note for each conversation: segment, current workflow, concrete
pain, essential integrations, task requested, objection, next commitment and
why it was accepted or declined. Keep names, private briefs and account material
outside Git in an approved research location. Obtain permission before recording.
This brief neither sends outreach nor collects participant or provider data.

After the first round, choose whether to continue the YouTube use case, narrow
the task, change the buyer hypothesis or pause a low-value direction. Update
this brief and the product overview from evidence. Update implementation issues
only when the chosen scope changes.

## Claims to use carefully

Say we are building the shared platform and can demonstrate the scoped local
workflows. Describe Bayesian learning as an explicit method for updating
beliefs from eligible evidence. Describe integrations as replaceable contracts
that still need provider-specific implementation and verification.

Do not claim proven acquisition uplift, autonomous ad buying, live YouTube
inventory/reporting, an online self-service release, enterprise readiness,
universal integrations or migrated agentic commerce. Simulated performance,
an LLM's confidence and an operator's attestation are different from verified
customer outcomes.
